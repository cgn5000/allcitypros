☀️ Solar Energy in Raleigh, NC

Costs, how to hire and what to ask · Updated October 2026

Solar can lower electricity costs for some homes, but the economics depend on your roof, your utility's rates and current incentives. This guide helps Raleigh homeowners compare solar installers and proposals.

Raleigh is in North Carolina, which generally has four distinct seasons with hot, humid summers and cool winters. That shapes what solar energy work looks like locally and when it is best to schedule it.

What to know in Raleigh

Solar economics in North Carolina depend heavily on your utility's net-metering rules and your roof. Get multiple proposals and compare the same assumptions across each.

How to hire the right solar installer in Raleigh

  1. Review recent electric bills to understand your usage and rates.
  2. Have your roof's age and condition assessed before installing panels.
  3. Get at least three proposals and compare them on a cost-per-watt and expected-production basis.
  4. Understand financing: cash purchase, loan, lease or power purchase agreement (PPA).
  5. Check which incentives currently apply — these change often — and how your utility credits excess power.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What is the total installed cost and cost per watt?
  • What production does the proposal assume, and how was it estimated?
  • Is this a purchase, loan, lease or PPA, and what are the escalators?
  • What are the warranties on panels, inverter and workmanship?
  • How does my utility credit excess power, and what could change?

Red flags to watch for

  • Claims of 'free solar' or guaranteed savings.
  • High-pressure same-day sign-up deals.
  • Proposals without production estimates.
  • Financing terms that aren't disclosed clearly.

Licensing and verification in North Carolina

Solar installers are generally required to hold appropriate contractor and electrical licenses, and systems need permits and utility interconnection approval. Verify with the North Carolina licensing authority. Incentives change often, so confirm current federal, state and utility programs from official sources.

When to schedule

Allow several weeks to months for design, permitting and utility approval. Start before the season when you expect the highest usage.

Frequently asked questions

Is solar worth it?
It depends on your electricity rates, sun exposure, roof and available incentives. Compare multiple proposals and run the numbers on your own bills.
Should I buy, lease or sign a PPA?
Purchasing usually has the best long-term return but requires upfront capital or a loan; leases and PPAs reduce upfront costs but have escalators and contract terms to review.
What happens if I sell my house?
Owned systems can add value; leases and PPAs usually need to be transferred. Ask each provider how they handle this.
