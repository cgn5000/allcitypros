License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Before you hire in Chicago: a checklist

  1. Look the plumber up in the official Illinois license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Illinois requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Chicago Department of Buildings and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our plumbing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Plumbing licensing in Illinois

Under the Illinois Plumbing License Law, IDPH licenses plumbers and apprentice plumbers, and plumbing contractors must register with the state and pay an annual fee. Plumbing contractors must carry general liability, bodily injury, property damage and workers' compensation insurance.

Building permits in Chicago

Residential building permits for Chicago are issued by City of Chicago Department of Buildings. Permits are commonly required for water heaters, gas lines and repiping. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Chicago's plumbing market: firms and pay

Census County Business Patterns counted 1,377 business locations with paid employees in Cook County in 2023 in the industry “plumbing, heating and air-conditioning contractors,” employing 15,401 people. The median annual wage for plumbers, pipefitters and steamfitters in the Chicago-Naperville-Elgin, IL-IN metro area was $103,380 (May 2025), higher than in most of the 108 metro areas we cover. Seasonal notes for Illinois

Frozen and burst pipes are the signature plumbing risk in Illinois. Insulate exposed lines, disconnect hoses before winter and keep your main shutoff easy to reach.
