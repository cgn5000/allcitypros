🪵 Flooring in Amarillo, TX

Costs, how to hire and what to ask · Updated October 2026

Flooring is both a major cost and a daily-use surface, so material choice and installation quality matter. This guide gives Amarillo homeowners a framework for comparing materials and installers.

Amarillo is in Texas, which generally has hot, humid summers and mild winters. That shapes what flooring work looks like locally and when it is best to schedule it.

What to know in Amarillo

Humidity in Texas can cause solid hardwood to cup or swell. Many homeowners choose luxury vinyl plank, tile or engineered wood and ask installers about moisture testing.

How to hire the right flooring installer in Amarillo

  1. Choose a material that fits your use, moisture exposure and budget.
  2. Measure carefully and ask installers to confirm measurements; order overage for waste.
  3. Get written quotes that separate material, labor, removal of old flooring and subfloor prep.
  4. Ask about moisture testing and acclimation for wood products.
  5. Plan for moving furniture and a few days of disruption.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Do you test subfloor flatness and moisture?
  • Is old flooring removal and disposal included?
  • What is the labor warranty?
  • Who handles manufacturer warranty claims?
  • How much overage do you recommend ordering?

Red flags to watch for

  • Quotes that exclude subfloor prep until after work begins.
  • No written scope or warranty.
  • Pressure to buy materials only through the installer.
  • Cash-only deals with no contract.

Licensing and verification in Texas

Flooring installers are not licensed everywhere, but contractors doing structural or tile work may be. Check requirements with the Texas licensing authority and verify insurance.

When to schedule

Order materials early because lead times vary, and let wood and some other products acclimate before installation.

Frequently asked questions

Which flooring is most durable?
Tile and luxury vinyl are popular for durability and moisture resistance; hardwood is durable and refinishable but sensitive to moisture.
How much extra material should I order?
Installers commonly recommend ordering roughly 5–15% extra depending on material and layout.
Can I install flooring myself?
Some floating floors are DIY-friendly. Tile, hardwood and complex layouts are usually worth a professional.
