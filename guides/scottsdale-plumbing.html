Before you hire in Scottsdale: a checklist

  1. Look the plumber up in the official Arizona license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Know the threshold: Unlicensed handyman work is allowed only for casual or minor jobs where the total contract price, including labor, materials, and all other items, is less than $1,000, and only if no building permit is required and the job is not part of a larger project; unlicensed workers must state "not a licensed contractor" in advertising. The exemption does not cover fire safety work or connections to natural gas, propane, or other fuel supplies.
  3. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  4. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Scottsdale Planning & Development, Permit Services and that final inspection is included in the price.
  5. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our plumbing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Plumbing licensing in Arizona

Arizona licenses plumbing contractors through the Registrar of Contractors (residential classification R-37, Plumbing). A license is required unless the job falls under the state's under-$1,000 handyman exemption.

Building permits in Scottsdale

Residential building permits for Scottsdale are issued by City of Scottsdale Planning & Development, Permit Services. Permits are commonly required for water heaters, gas lines and repiping. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Seasonal notes for Arizona

Hard water is common in dry climates and builds scale in water heaters and fixtures. Ask a plumber about softening or periodic water-heater flushing.
