❄️ HVAC in Reno, NV

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for HVAC work in Reno? Start with the facts that are specific to Reno and Nevada: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Reno: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official Nevada license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Know the threshold: The state's small-repair exemption (under $1,000 including labor and materials) does not apply to plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, heating, or air-conditioning work, so this work requires a license regardless of job size.
  3. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Nevada requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  4. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Reno Development Services Department and that final inspection is included in the price.
  5. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Nevada

Nevada licenses refrigeration and air-conditioning contractors through the Nevada State Contractors Board (classification C-21, Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning). Contractors must be licensed before bidding or performing work.

Who licenses itNevada State Contractors Board (NSCB)
When a license is requiredThe state's small-repair exemption (under $1,000 including labor and materials) does not apply to plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, heating, or air-conditioning work, so this work requires a license regardless of job size
Insurance / bondBond amount is set by the Board at approval and can range from $1,000 to $500,000
Check a licenseOfficial license lookup (app.nvcontractorsboard.com)

Building permits in Reno

Residential building permits for Reno are issued by City of Reno Development Services Department. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Reno climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Reno has more heating degree days than 113 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 38. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Nevada

Cooling dominates in Nevada: dust, extreme heat and long run-times shorten equipment life. Ask about filter changes, coil cleaning and whether the system is rated for high outdoor temperatures.
