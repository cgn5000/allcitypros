🎨 Painting in St. Paul, MN

Costs, how to hire and what to ask · Updated October 2026

Painting results depend more on preparation than on the paint. This guide shows St. Paul homeowners what typical projects cost and how to compare painter proposals fairly.

St. Paul is in Minnesota, which generally has cold winters, snow or hard freezes, and warm summers. That shapes what painting work looks like locally and when it is best to schedule it.

What to know in St. Paul

Painting seasons are short in Minnesota. Exterior work needs a stretch of warm, dry days, so book early for summer.

How to hire the right painter in St. Paul

  1. Decide the scope: rooms, trim, ceilings, exterior surfaces and any repairs.
  2. Get three written proposals that list prep work, number of coats and the paint brand and line.
  3. Ask how the crew protects floors and furniture and how they handle lead-safe practices in older homes.
  4. Check insurance and references, and ask to see recent work.
  5. Agree on the color, finish and schedule in writing.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What prep work is included (scraping, sanding, caulking, priming)?
  • Which paint brand and product line will you use?
  • How many coats are included?
  • Do you follow lead-safe work practices for homes built before 1978?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover?

Red flags to watch for

  • Proposals that don't mention prep or paint brand.
  • Very low bids that cut coats or prep.
  • Large deposits before any work.
  • No insurance or references.

Licensing and verification in Minnesota

Painting requirements vary: some states license painters or require certification for lead-safe renovation (EPA RRP) in pre-1978 homes. Check with the Minnesota licensing authority.

When to schedule

Interior painting can happen year-round; exterior projects need suitable weather, so book ahead for the best seasons.

Frequently asked questions

How long does exterior paint last?
Quality exterior paint and good prep can last many years, though sun, moisture and surface condition all matter.
Do I need to buy the paint?
Usually the painter supplies it, but you can specify brand and color. Make sure the proposal states what is included.
What's the cheapest way to save money?
Do simple prep (moving furniture, removing wall hangings) yourself and consolidate trim and ceiling work into a single project.
