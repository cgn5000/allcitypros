Before you hire in Jersey City: a checklist

  1. Look the roofing contractor up in the official New Jersey license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond New Jersey requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Jersey City Division of the Construction Code Official and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our roofing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Roofing licensing in New Jersey

New Jersey has no separate roofing license board; contractors doing residential home improvement work, including roofing, must register with the Division of Consumer Affairs as Home Improvement Contractor Businesses.

Building permits in Jersey City

Residential building permits for Jersey City are issued by City of Jersey City Division of the Construction Code Official. Permits are commonly required for re-roofing and structural roof repairs. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Jersey City climate and what it means for your roof

Jersey City averages 46.6 inches of precipitation a year, more than 118 of the 153 cities we have this figure for, and about 32 inches of snow, so ask about ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys and the roof's snow-load rating. With 1,270 cooling degree days, heat and UV exposure are a moderate factor; with 4,701 heating degree days, freeze-thaw cycles and ice dams deserve attention.

Seasonal notes for New Jersey

Snow load, ice dams and freeze-thaw damage drive roofing work in New Jersey. Attic insulation and ventilation are as important as the shingles themselves.
