🏠 Roofing in Honolulu, HI

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for roofing work in Honolulu? Start with the facts that are specific to Honolulu and Hawaii: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Honolulu: a checklist

  1. Look the roofing contractor up in the official Hawaii license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Hawaii requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City and County of Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting (DPP) and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our roofing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Roofing licensing in Hawaii

Hawaii licenses roofers through the Contractors License Board under the C-42 roofing contractor specialty classification. Licensed contractors must carry liability and workers' compensation insurance.

Insurance / bondLiability insurance of at least $100,000 bodily injury per person, $300,000 per occurrence, and $50,000 property damage per occurrence, plus workers' compensation insurance.

Building permits in Honolulu

Residential building permits for Honolulu are issued by City and County of Honolulu Department of Planning and Permitting (DPP). Permits are commonly required for re-roofing and structural roof repairs. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Seasonal notes for Hawaii

Intense sun, heavy rain and wind-driven storms are the main stressors on Hawaii roofs. Ask about wind-rated shingles, proper underlayment and ventilation, and photograph storm damage before calling your insurer.
