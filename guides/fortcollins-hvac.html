Hiring for HVAC work in Fort Collins? Start with the facts that are specific to Fort Collins and Colorado: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Fort Collins: a checklist

  1. Colorado has no statewide license for this work, so ask which local license or registration the HVAC contractor holds and confirm it with the issuing city or county.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Fort Collins Building Services and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Colorado

Colorado does not issue a statewide HVAC contractor license. Mechanical/HVAC contractor licensing is handled by cities and counties, so homeowners should check with their city or county building department.

Who licenses itNo statewide license in Colorado; cities and counties set the rules

Building permits in Fort Collins

Residential building permits for Fort Collins are issued by City of Fort Collins Building Services. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Fort Collins climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Fort Collins has more heating degree days than 134 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 16. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Colorado

Heating reliability is the priority in Colorado. Schedule furnace or boiler service before the first hard freeze, and ask about sizing, venting and backup options if your system fails in a cold snap.
