⚡ Electrical in Denver, CO

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for electrical work in Denver? Start with the facts that are specific to Denver and Colorado: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Denver: a checklist

  1. Look the electrician up in the official Colorado license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City and County of Denver Community Planning and Development and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our electrical guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Electrical licensing in Colorado

The Colorado State Electrical Board, part of the Department of Regulatory Agencies, licenses residential wiremen, journeyman electricians and master electricians. Homeowners can verify a license with DORA's online license lookup.

Building permits in Denver

Residential building permits for Denver are issued by City and County of Denver Community Planning and Development. Permits are commonly required for panel upgrades, new circuits and EV chargers. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Denver's electrical market: firms and pay

Census County Business Patterns counted 158 business locations with paid employees in Denver County in 2023 in the industry “electrical contractors and other wiring installation contractors,” employing 3,069 people. The median annual wage for electricians in the Denver-Aurora-Centennial, CO metro area was $63,150 (May 2025), close to the middle of the 108 metro areas we cover.
