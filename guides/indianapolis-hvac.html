Hiring for HVAC work in Indianapolis? Start with the facts that are specific to Indianapolis and Indiana: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Indianapolis: a checklist

  1. Indiana has no statewide license for this work, so ask which local license or registration the HVAC contractor holds and confirm it with the issuing city or county.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Indianapolis / Marion County Department of Business and Neighborhood Services (BNS) and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Indiana

Indiana has no statewide licensing requirement for HVAC technicians or contractors; licensing is handled by counties and cities, and most local governments require a license to operate as an HVAC contractor. Homeowners should check with their city or county building department.

Building permits in Indianapolis

Residential building permits for Indianapolis are issued by City of Indianapolis / Marion County Department of Business and Neighborhood Services (BNS). Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Indianapolis climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Indianapolis has more heating degree days than 123 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 48. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Indiana

Heating reliability is the priority in Indiana. Schedule furnace or boiler service before the first hard freeze, and ask about sizing, venting and backup options if your system fails in a cold snap.
