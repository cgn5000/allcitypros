🚛 Moving in Yonkers, NY

Costs, how to hire and what to ask · Updated October 2026

Moving is stressful and a common source of consumer complaints. This guide explains typical costs, how to verify a mover and how to protect your belongings when moving from or within Yonkers.

Yonkers is in New York, which generally has cold winters, snow or hard freezes, and warm summers. That shapes what moving work looks like locally and when it is best to schedule it.

What to know in Yonkers

Winter moves in New York can mean snow and ice. Ask about weather policies, and book early for summer when demand peaks.

How to hire the right moving company in Yonkers

  1. Take inventory and decide what you will pack yourself.
  2. Get in-home or video estimates from at least three licensed movers.
  3. Compare binding versus non-binding estimates and what's included.
  4. Verify registration: interstate movers must have a USDOT number, and many states regulate local movers.
  5. Review the valuation (insurance) options and get everything in writing.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Are you licensed and registered, and what is your USDOT number?
  • Is the estimate binding, not-to-exceed or hourly?
  • What valuation (insurance) coverage is included?
  • Are fuel, stairs, long carry or packing materials extra?
  • How do you handle delays and damage claims?

Red flags to watch for

  • Large deposits or cash-only demands.
  • Phone-only quotes without an inventory.
  • No company name on trucks or paperwork.
  • Estimates far below others.

Licensing and verification in New York

Interstate movers must be registered with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration and hold a USDOT number. Local moves are regulated by New York in many states. Verify both before you book.

When to schedule

Book several weeks ahead; late spring through summer and end-of-month dates are busiest and most expensive.

Frequently asked questions

How much do movers cost?
Local moves are commonly priced hourly and can range from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars; long-distance moves cost more. Always compare written estimates.
How do I check that a moving company is legitimate?
Look up their USDOT number for interstate moves, check state registration, and read recent customer reviews.
Do I need moving insurance?
Basic carrier liability is limited. Ask about full-value protection or third-party coverage for valuable items.
