🏠 Roofing in Springfield, IL

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for roofing work in Springfield? Start with the facts that are specific to Springfield and Illinois: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Springfield: a checklist

  1. Look the roofing contractor up in the official Illinois license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Illinois requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Springfield Office of Public Works, Building & Zoning and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our roofing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Roofing licensing in Illinois

Under the Illinois Roofing Industry Licensing Act, it is unlawful to work as a roofing contractor without an IDFPR license. Applicants must post a bond and have a qualifying party who passed the state exam.

Insurance / bondContinuous bond of $10,000 for a limited license and $25,000 for an unlimited license.

Building permits in Springfield

Residential building permits for Springfield are issued by City of Springfield Office of Public Works, Building & Zoning. Permits are commonly required for re-roofing and structural roof repairs. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Springfield climate and what it means for your roof

Springfield averages 38.0 inches of precipitation a year, more than 82 of the 153 cities we have this figure for, and about 22 inches of snow, so ask about ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys and the roof's snow-load rating. With 1,252 cooling degree days, heat and UV exposure are a moderate factor; with 5,215 heating degree days, freeze-thaw cycles and ice dams deserve attention.

Seasonal notes for Illinois

Snow load, ice dams and freeze-thaw damage drive roofing work in Illinois. Attic insulation and ventilation are as important as the shingles themselves.
