Before you hire in Oklahoma City: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official Oklahoma license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Oklahoma City Development Services Department and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Oklahoma

Oklahoma's Construction Industries Board licenses mechanical (HVAC) contractors and journeymen, with an apprentice path. Contractors must carry general liability insurance, and bond and insurance (or an approved waiver affidavit) are required to activate a contractor license.

Building permits in Oklahoma City

Residential building permits for Oklahoma City are issued by City of Oklahoma City Development Services Department. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Oklahoma City climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating and cooling demand are both significant here. Oklahoma City has more heating degree days than 93 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 94. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Oklahoma

Oklahoma homes need both a dependable air conditioner and a heating system. Heat pumps are a common option in this climate; ask installers to compare a heat pump with a conventional furnace and AC for your home.
