🔨 General Contracting in Chicago, IL

Costs, how to hire and what to ask · Updated October 2026

A remodel is likely to be one of the largest checks you write for your home. This guide explains how to scope a project, compare general contractors in Chicago and keep the job on budget.

Chicago is in Illinois, which generally has cold winters, snow or hard freezes, and warm summers. That shapes what general contracting work looks like locally and when it is best to schedule it.

What to know in Chicago

Construction seasons in Illinois are shaped by winter. Ask how weather delays are handled in the contract.

How to hire the right general contractor in Chicago

  1. Define the scope and budget, including a contingency of 10–20% for surprises.
  2. Collect three or more detailed bids with itemized costs and clear allowances.
  3. Check license, insurance, references and recent project photos.
  4. Confirm who pulls permits, and the schedule for inspections.
  5. Use a written contract with a payment schedule tied to completed work, not dates.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Are you licensed in Illinois and insured?
  • Who is on site day-to-day, and how often will you update me?
  • What is included in each allowance, and how are overages handled?
  • How are change orders priced and approved?
  • Will you provide lien waivers as you are paid?

Red flags to watch for

  • A large up-front payment far beyond a modest deposit.
  • No written contract or vague scope.
  • No permits for work that requires them.
  • Reluctance to provide references or insurance.

Licensing and verification in Illinois

Contractor licensing is handled at the state level in many states and locally in others, and permits are generally required for structural, electrical and plumbing work. Verify with the Illinois licensing authority and the Chicago building department.

When to schedule

Good contractors book out weeks or months. Start planning and collecting bids well before you want work to begin.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I put down?
Many states cap deposits; ask your state's rules and tie later payments to progress. Avoid paying the majority up front.
What is a change order?
A written modification to the scope and price. Insist that all changes be agreed in writing before work proceeds.
Do I need permits?
Many remodels do. A reputable contractor will pull permits in their own name and schedule inspections.
