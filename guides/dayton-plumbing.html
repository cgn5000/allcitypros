Before you hire in Dayton: a checklist

  1. Look the plumber up in the official Ohio license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Dayton Department of Planning, Neighborhoods & Development, Building Inspection Division and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our plumbing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Plumbing licensing in Ohio

Ohio's OCILB licenses plumbing and hydronics contractors for commercial work under Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4740. Residential contractor regulation is left to local jurisdictions, which may require the state license at their discretion, so homeowners should also check with their city or county.

Building permits in Dayton

Residential building permits for Dayton are issued by City of Dayton Department of Planning, Neighborhoods & Development, Building Inspection Division. Permits are commonly required for water heaters, gas lines and repiping. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Seasonal notes for Ohio

Frozen and burst pipes are the signature plumbing risk in Ohio. Insulate exposed lines, disconnect hoses before winter and keep your main shutoff easy to reach.
