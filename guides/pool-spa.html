🏊 Pool & Spa: Costs & How to Hire

What the work costs, how to compare quotes and what to ask · Updated October 2026

Pool ownership is a recurring commitment. This guide explains typical service costs, what to look for in your area pool companies and how to avoid common surprises.

National ballpark ranges for planning only — not quotes. What you pay depends on scope, materials, access and your local market, so always compare written estimates.

How to hire the right pool service company

  1. Decide whether you want weekly service, a one-time repair or a build or renovation.
  2. Get quotes that spell out chemicals, visit frequency and what's excluded.
  3. For builds and renovations, request a detailed contract, drawings and a payment schedule.
  4. Check licensing and insurance, and ask for local references.
  5. Confirm who handles permits, fencing and safety requirements.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Are chemicals included in the monthly price?
  • How often will you visit, and do you test water each time?
  • Are you licensed and insured in your state?
  • How are repairs quoted and approved?
  • Who handles permits and safety barriers for new pools?

Red flags to watch for

  • No written service agreement.
  • Payment for a large build mostly up front.
  • Unlicensed work on electrical or gas equipment.
  • Vague pricing for 'extras.'

Licensing and verification

Pool contractors are often licensed at the state or local level, and building a pool typically requires permits and safety barriers. Verify with your state's licensing authority and your local building department.

When to schedule

Book openings and major repairs ahead of the swim season; off-season is often a good time for renovation scheduling.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pool maintenance cost?
Full-service weekly maintenance often runs between $100 and $250 per month depending on pool size and market. Chemicals may be extra.
Should I service the pool myself?
Many owners do basic skimming and testing, but chemical balance and equipment checks are good jobs for a professional.
What safety rules apply?
Most places require barriers or fencing for pools. Check local requirements before building.
