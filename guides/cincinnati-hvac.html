Before you hire in Cincinnati: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official Ohio license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Cincinnati Department of Buildings & Inspections and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Ohio

Ohio's OCILB licenses heating, ventilating and air conditioning (HVAC) and refrigeration contractors for commercial work under Ohio Revised Code Chapter 4740. Residential contractor regulation is left to local jurisdictions, which may require the state license at their discretion, so homeowners should also check with their city or county.

Building permits in Cincinnati

Residential building permits for Cincinnati are issued by City of Cincinnati Department of Buildings & Inspections. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Cincinnati climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Cincinnati has more heating degree days than 116 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 47. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Ohio

Heating reliability is the priority in Ohio. Schedule furnace or boiler service before the first hard freeze, and ask about sizing, venting and backup options if your system fails in a cold snap.
