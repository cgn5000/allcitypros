Hiring for electrical work in El Paso? Start with the facts that are specific to El Paso and Texas: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in El Paso: a checklist

  1. Look the electrician up in the official Texas license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of El Paso Planning & Inspections Department and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our electrical guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Electrical licensing in Texas

Texas requires anyone performing non-exempt electrical work to be licensed by TDLR and to work through a licensed electrical contractor.

Building permits in El Paso

Residential building permits for El Paso are issued by City of El Paso Planning & Inspections Department. Permits are commonly required for panel upgrades, new circuits and EV chargers. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

El Paso's electrical market: firms and pay

Census County Business Patterns counted 160 business locations with paid employees in El Paso County in 2023 in the industry “electrical contractors and other wiring installation contractors,” employing 1,923 people. The median annual wage for electricians in the El Paso, TX metro area was $48,140 (May 2025), lower than in most of the 108 metro areas we cover.
