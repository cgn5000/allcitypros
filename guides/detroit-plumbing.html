🔧 Plumbing in Detroit, MI

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for plumbing work in Detroit? Start with the facts that are specific to Detroit and Michigan: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Detroit: a checklist

  1. Look the plumber up in the official Michigan license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Detroit Buildings, Safety Engineering, and Environmental Department (BSEED) and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our plumbing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Plumbing licensing in Michigan

Michigan licenses journey plumbers, master plumbers and plumbing contractors through LARA's Bureau of Construction Codes. Homeowners can verify a license in the bureau's online license lookup.

Building permits in Detroit

Residential building permits for Detroit are issued by City of Detroit Buildings, Safety Engineering, and Environmental Department (BSEED). Permits are commonly required for water heaters, gas lines and repiping. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Detroit's plumbing market: firms and pay

Census County Business Patterns counted 301 business locations with paid employees in Wayne County in 2023 in the industry “plumbing, heating and air-conditioning contractors,” employing 3,293 people. The median annual wage for plumbers, pipefitters and steamfitters in the Detroit-Warren-Dearborn, MI metro area was $86,370 (May 2025), higher than in most of the 108 metro areas we cover. Seasonal notes for Michigan

Frozen and burst pipes are the signature plumbing risk in Michigan. Insulate exposed lines, disconnect hoses before winter and keep your main shutoff easy to reach.
