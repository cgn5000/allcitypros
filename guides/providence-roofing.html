🏠 Roofing in Providence, RI

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for roofing work in Providence? Start with the facts that are specific to Providence and Rhode Island: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Providence: a checklist

  1. Look the roofing contractor up in the official Rhode Island license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Rhode Island requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Providence Department of Inspection and Standards and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our roofing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Roofing licensing in Rhode Island

Rhode Island requires a Commercial Roofing License from the CRLB for commercial or industrial roofing work; it does not cover residential buildings with four or fewer dwelling units. Residential roofers must still register with the CRLB as contractors.

Building permits in Providence

Residential building permits for Providence are issued by City of Providence Department of Inspection and Standards. Permits are commonly required for re-roofing and structural roof repairs. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Providence climate and what it means for your roof

Providence averages 47.5 inches of precipitation a year, more than 123 of the 153 cities we have this figure for, and about 37 inches of snow, so ask about ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys and the roof's snow-load rating. With 812 cooling degree days, heat and UV exposure are a moderate factor; with 5,478 heating degree days, freeze-thaw cycles and ice dams deserve attention.

Seasonal notes for Rhode Island

Snow load, ice dams and freeze-thaw damage drive roofing work in Rhode Island. Attic insulation and ventilation are as important as the shingles themselves.
