License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for plumbing work in Richmond? Start with the facts that are specific to Richmond and Virginia: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Richmond: a checklist

  1. Look the plumber up in the official Virginia license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Know the threshold: Class C contractor license required for a single contract or project over $1,000 but less than $30,000 (and less than $250,000 within any 12-month period); Class B and Class A apply to larger amounts.
  3. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  4. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Richmond Planning and Development Review, Bureau of Permits and Inspections and that final inspection is included in the price.
  5. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our plumbing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Plumbing licensing in Virginia

Virginia's Board for Contractors licenses individual plumbers at journeyman and master levels, with a separate residential plumbing license. Firms doing plumbing work over $1,000 also need a Class A, B, or C contractor license.

Building permits in Richmond

Residential building permits for Richmond are issued by City of Richmond Planning and Development Review, Bureau of Permits and Inspections. Permits are commonly required for water heaters, gas lines and repiping. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Seasonal notes for Virginia

Occasional hard freezes in Virginia can burst exposed pipes. Insulate outdoor spigots and pipes in unheated spaces and know where your main shutoff is.
