Before you hire in Des Moines: a checklist

  1. Know the threshold: Registration required for contractors earning $2,000 or more per year from construction.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Iowa requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Des Moines Permit and Development Center and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our roofing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Roofing licensing in Iowa

Iowa has no roofing-specific license; roofers are covered by the state's construction contractor registration, which applies to contractors earning at least $2,000 a year from construction work.

Who licenses itIowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (DIAL) – Contractor Registration
When a license is requiredRegistration required for contractors earning $2,000 or more per year from construction.
Insurance / bondContractors with employees must carry workers' compensation and file a $25,000 bond; out-of-state contractors must file the bond regardless.

Source: dial.iowa.gov, checked November 15, 2026. Rules change; confirm with the agency before you sign a contract.

Building permits in Des Moines

Residential building permits for Des Moines are issued by City of Des Moines Permit and Development Center. Permits are commonly required for re-roofing and structural roof repairs. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Des Moines climate and what it means for your roof

Des Moines averages 36.5 inches of precipitation a year, more than 74 of the 153 cities we have this figure for, and about 36 inches of snow, so ask about ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys and the roof's snow-load rating. With 1,070 cooling degree days, heat and UV exposure are a moderate factor; with 6,178 heating degree days, freeze-thaw cycles and ice dams deserve attention.

Seasonal notes for Iowa

Snow load, ice dams and freeze-thaw damage drive roofing work in Iowa. Attic insulation and ventilation are as important as the shingles themselves.
