🔧 Plumbing in Springfield, MO

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for plumbing work in Springfield? Start with the facts that are specific to Springfield and Missouri: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Springfield: a checklist

  1. Missouri has no statewide license for this work, so ask which local license or registration the plumber holds and confirm it with the issuing city or county.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Springfield Building Development Services and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our plumbing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Plumbing licensing in Missouri

Missouri does not issue a statewide plumbing contractor license. Licensing, where required, is handled by cities and counties, so homeowners should check with their city or county building department.

Who licenses itNo statewide license in Missouri; cities and counties set the rules

Building permits in Springfield

Residential building permits for Springfield are issued by City of Springfield Building Development Services. Permits are commonly required for water heaters, gas lines and repiping. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Seasonal notes for Missouri

Occasional hard freezes in Missouri can burst exposed pipes. Insulate outdoor spigots and pipes in unheated spaces and know where your main shutoff is.
