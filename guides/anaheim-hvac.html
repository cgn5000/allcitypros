Before you hire in Anaheim: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official California license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Know the threshold: License required when labor, materials, and all other costs total $1,000 or more, or when the job requires a permit or the person hires workers (effective January 1, 2025).
  3. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond California requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  4. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Anaheim Building Division and that final inspection is included in the price.
  5. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in California

California licenses heating and air-conditioning contractors through the CSLB under the C-20 (Warm-Air Heating, Ventilating and Air-Conditioning) classification. Since January 1, 2025, a license in the appropriate classification is required for jobs of $1,000 or more, and for smaller jobs that need a building permit or use hired workers.

Building permits in Anaheim

Residential building permits for Anaheim are issued by City of Anaheim Building Division. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Seasonal notes for California

With a milder climate, many California homes run heating and cooling less often, which makes efficiency upgrades slower to pay back. Heat pumps and ductless systems are worth pricing alongside traditional equipment.
