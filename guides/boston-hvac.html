Before you hire in Boston: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official Massachusetts license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Boston Inspectional Services Department and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Massachusetts

Massachusetts has no single HVAC license; HVAC work falls under several state licenses. Sheet metal work such as ductwork must be done by a master or journeyperson sheet metal worker licensed by the Board of Examiners of Sheet Metal Workers, with a local permit, and refrigeration technicians are licensed separately by the Division of Occupational Licensure.

Building permits in Boston

Residential building permits for Boston are issued by City of Boston Inspectional Services Department. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Boston climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Boston has more heating degree days than 129 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 24. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Massachusetts

Heating reliability is the priority in Massachusetts. Schedule furnace or boiler service before the first hard freeze, and ask about sizing, venting and backup options if your system fails in a cold snap.
