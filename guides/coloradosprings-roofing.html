🏠 Roofing in Colorado Springs, CO

License checks, permits, local firms and pay · Sources checked November 2026

Hiring for roofing work in Colorado Springs? Start with the facts that are specific to Colorado Springs and Colorado: who licenses the work, how to check a license, where permits come from, and what the local market looks like. Every figure below links to its official source.

Before you hire in Colorado Springs: a checklist

  1. Colorado has no statewide license for this work, so ask which local license or registration the roofing contractor holds and confirm it with the issuing city or county.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with Pikes Peak Regional Building Department (serves Colorado Springs and El Paso County) and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our roofing guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Roofing licensing in Colorado

Colorado does not issue a statewide roofing contractor license. Roofing contractor licensing, where required, is handled by cities and counties, so homeowners should check with their city or county building department.

Who licenses itNo statewide license in Colorado; cities and counties set the rules

Building permits in Colorado Springs

Residential building permits for Colorado Springs are issued by Pikes Peak Regional Building Department (serves Colorado Springs and El Paso County). Permits are commonly required for re-roofing and structural roof repairs. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Colorado Springs climate and what it means for your roof

Colorado Springs averages 15.9 inches of precipitation a year, more than 42 of the 153 cities we have this figure for, and about 32 inches of snow, so ask about ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys and the roof's snow-load rating. With 608 cooling degree days, heat and UV exposure are a moderate factor; with 5,888 heating degree days, freeze-thaw cycles and ice dams deserve attention.

Seasonal notes for Colorado

Snow load, ice dams and freeze-thaw damage drive roofing work in Colorado. Attic insulation and ventilation are as important as the shingles themselves.
