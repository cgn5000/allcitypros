Before you hire in Louisville: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official Kentucky license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage before work starts.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with Louisville Metro Government Office of Construction Review and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Kentucky

Kentucky licenses heating and air conditioning contractors through DHBC's Division of HVAC, which issues master HVAC contractor and journeyman HVAC mechanic licenses. Only properly licensed or authorized individuals may pull HVAC permits.

Building permits in Louisville

Residential building permits for Louisville are issued by Louisville Metro Government Office of Construction Review. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Louisville climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Louisville has more heating degree days than 102 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 72. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Kentucky

Kentucky homes need both a dependable air conditioner and a heating system. Heat pumps are a common option in this climate; ask installers to compare a heat pump with a conventional furnace and AC for your home.
