Before you hire in Des Moines: a checklist

  1. Look the HVAC contractor up in the official Iowa license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Iowa requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  3. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Des Moines Permit and Development Center and that final inspection is included in the price.
  4. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our HVAC guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

HVAC licensing in Iowa

Iowa's Plumbing and Mechanical Systems Board licenses HVAC, refrigeration, hydronic, sheet metal and mechanical contractors and tradespeople. A contracting business must hold a PMSB contractor license with a master licensee of record for each discipline it performs.

Who licenses itIowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (DIAL) – Plumbing and Mechanical Systems Board
Insurance / bondLicensed contractors must carry at least $500,000 in public liability insurance and a $5,000 surety bond.

Building permits in Des Moines

Residential building permits for Des Moines are issued by City of Des Moines Permit and Development Center. Permits are commonly required for replacing or adding heating and cooling equipment. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.

Des Moines climate and what it means for heating and cooling

Heating demand is far larger than cooling demand here. Des Moines has more heating degree days than 142 of the 155 cities we cover and more cooling degree days than 42. Degree days track how hard a system works over a year, so they are a fair guide to whether to prioritize furnace or heat-pump heating capacity, cooling capacity and dehumidification, and how quickly an efficiency upgrade pays back.

Seasonal notes for Iowa

Heating reliability is the priority in Iowa. Schedule furnace or boiler service before the first hard freeze, and ask about sizing, venting and backup options if your system fails in a cold snap.
