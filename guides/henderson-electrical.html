Before you hire in Henderson: a checklist

  1. Look the electrician up in the official Nevada license lookup and make sure the name and license number match the contract.
  2. Know the threshold: The state's small-repair exemption (under $1,000 including labor and materials) does not apply to plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, heating, or air-conditioning work, so this work requires a license regardless of job size.
  3. Ask for proof of the insurance or bond Nevada requires (listed below) and current workers' compensation coverage.
  4. Confirm who will pull the permit with City of Henderson Building and Fire Safety Department and that final inspection is included in the price.
  5. Get at least three written quotes for the same scope; our electrical guide covers typical costs, questions to ask and red flags.

Electrical licensing in Nevada

Nevada licenses electrical contractors through the Nevada State Contractors Board (classification C-2, Electrical). Contractors must be licensed before bidding or performing work.

Who licenses itNevada State Contractors Board (NSCB)
When a license is requiredThe state's small-repair exemption (under $1,000 including labor and materials) does not apply to plumbing, electrical, refrigeration, heating, or air-conditioning work, so this work requires a license regardless of job size
Insurance / bondBond amount is set by the Board at approval and can range from $1,000 to $500,000

Building permits in Henderson

Residential building permits for Henderson are issued by City of Henderson Building and Fire Safety Department. Permits are commonly required for panel upgrades, new circuits and EV chargers. Ask your contractor who will pull the permit and book the inspections; a contractor who asks you to pull it yourself is a warning sign.
